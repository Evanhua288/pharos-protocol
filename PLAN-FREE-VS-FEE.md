# The specification is free. What is billed is a task being done.

A fair question: if the specification is free, why would anyone pay a metering
fee?

## The short answer

The specification is free. What is billed is the execution, confirmation, and
metering of a specific task, together with the evidence that it happened.

## Why these are different things

| Item | Who pays | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Spec text, reference implementation, vectors | nobody | published under CC BY 4.0 and Apache License 2.0 |
| A task actually carried out for you | the party ordering it | per-task metering fee, unit cost times 1.20 |
| A member usage certificate | the holder | fixed small issuance fee, unrelated to volume |

Using the specification costs nothing. Having work done, and having it
confirmed and accounted for, is a service.

## What that service actually is

An ordinary delivery has three weaknesses, and all three are what is being
paid for:

1. Nobody can say clearly whether the delivery happened.
   We produce a jointly confirmed record.
2. Every party keeps its own numbers and they disagree.
   We produce a metering record with a reproducible trace.
3. When asked to prove it, no evidence can be produced.
   We produce a proof ledger and consistency proofs that any third party can
   recompute.

## What is not charged

- No charge for reading, implementing, or redistributing the specification.
- No charge for using the name of the protocol, for any conforming
  implementation.
- No platform fee, no service fee, no implementation licence fee.
- No charge levied on another party's revenue.

## What is never said

We never claim that anyone is compliant, mutually recognised, approved, or
certified. A reproduced result evidences agreement with the specification
and nothing more.

This batch of output evidences the following: our kernel implementation is
consistent with the published specification. It does not evidence that we
are compliant.
